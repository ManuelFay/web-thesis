\chapter{The Thesis in Twenty Terms}
\label{appendix:chapter-tfidf}

As a small application of the lexical retrieval methods discussed in this
thesis, this chapter gives a compact view of the vocabulary that most
distinguishes each main-body chapter. The lists below contain the twenty terms
with the highest term frequency--inverse document frequency (TF--IDF) weight in
each chapter \sidecite{sparck_jones_statistical_1972}.

The computation treats each of the eight main-body chapters as one document.
The accompanying script, \texttt{scripts/chapter\_tfidf.py}, removes comments,
LaTeX commands, citations, references and mathematics; normalizes words to
lowercase unaccented forms; removes English stopwords and common document
scaffolding; excludes presentation-only vocabulary introduced by the source
formatting rather than the prose; and retains unigram terms of at least three
characters. These exclusions are deliberately limited to generic structural
or formatting artifacts rather than topic-specific words. For a term $t$ in
chapter $d$, it then computes
\[
  \operatorname{tfidf}(t,d)
  =
  \frac{f_{t,d}}{\sum_{t'} f_{t',d}}
  \left(
    \log\frac{1 + N}{1 + n_t} + 1
  \right),
\]
where $N=8$ is the number of chapters and $n_t$ is the number of chapters
containing $t$. The smoothing avoids zero weights, and alphabetical order
breaks exact score ties. The generated lists can be reproduced with
\texttt{python3 scripts/chapter\_tfidf.py}.
