\chapter{The Thesis in Twenty Terms}
\label{appendix:chapter-tfidf}

As a small application of the lexical retrieval methods discussed in this
thesis, this chapter gives a compact view of the vocabulary that most
distinguishes each main-body chapter. The lists below contain the twenty terms
with the highest term frequency--inverse document frequency (TF--IDF) weight in
each chapter \sidecite{sparck_jones_statistical_1972}. TF--IDF and other
recurring technical terms are defined in \autoref{ch:glossary}.

The computation treats each of the eight main-body chapters as one document.
The text is normalized by removing comments, LaTeX commands, citations,
references, mathematics, English stopwords, and common document scaffolding.
Words are converted to lowercase unaccented forms, and unigram terms of at
least three characters are retained. These exclusions are deliberately limited
to generic structural or formatting artifacts rather than topic-specific words.
For a term $t$ in chapter $d$, the weight is
\[
  \operatorname{tfidf}(t,d)
  =
  \frac{f_{t,d}}{\sum_{t'} f_{t',d}}
  \left(
    \log\frac{1 + N}{1 + n_t} + 1
  \right),
\]
where $N=8$ is the number of chapters and $n_t$ is the number of chapters
containing $t$. The smoothing avoids zero weights, and alphabetical order
breaks exact score ties.
